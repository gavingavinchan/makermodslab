# General — Nut-threading operating notes

These notes are advisory; the next workspace's README governs commands, sensors, limits, and profiles.

## General — Success, seating, and evidence

- Task success requires at least two **relative nut-minus-bolt turns** while threaded; seating is separate.
  Hand roll, requested roll, and world nut rotation are not interchangeable with relative thread rotation.
- Track actual nut rotation relative to the bolt and axial advance relative to the bolt, not just TCP feed.
  For a 3.5 mm pitch, two relative turns imply about 7 mm advance before seating; verify the current pitch.
- Seating needs evidence that the nut's bearing face has reached the flange, after confirmed thread advance.
  Bolt protrusion or a nut staying on the thread after release establishes neither a closed gap nor seating.
- A collision, hooked finger, joint limit, or otherwise loaded wrist can mimic a snug torque stall.
  Inspect the gap from a useful view, clearance, residuals, and loads; if occluded, report seating as uncertain.
- Scorer measurements below are retrospective evidence, unavailable to the live operator. `done` records a claim.

## General — Prepare a reliable first attempt

1. Inspect before every decision and after every motion. Identify the nut, exposed bolt, flange bearing face,
   approach corridor, and whether the bolt needs restraint. Use the other wrist view when the working view is occluded.
2. Measure opposing face edges for the nut center and three non-collinear points on the same face for its normal.
   Measure the exposed shaft/tip and flange separately; a depth sample can hit a bevel, finger, or background in the bore.
   Refresh calibration after movement; depth queries may advance time.
3. Grip opposing outer flats with pad overlap, avoiding the bore and inner lip. Align the actual bore with both
   the bolt axis and wrist roll axis; a straight wrist does not prove a straight nut. Recheck after any handoff.
4. Choose a reachable staging posture with wrist margin and clearance for the full tightening/reset cycle.
   Account for fingertips, open jaws, wrist camera, and forearm, not only the TCP. Test small rolls in clear space.
5. Approach from the tip toward the flange with near-parallel axes and preferably submillimetre centering.
   Use 1–2 mm final nudges; inspect reached motion and stop at unexpected resistance or contact loss.
6. Touch the leading face to the tip without pushing through resistance. For the documented tip-side convention,
   positive roll/feed tighten: feed = relative degrees × pitch / 360. With 3.5 mm pitch, +20° needs +0.194 mm.
   This hand-feed pairing assumes the bolt stays still. A clean hand turn alone does not prove thread engagement.
7. Verify nut-relative rotation and matching advance before automatic release/reset cycles. Start with a short,
   inspected screw batch; extend only after consistent progress. Reduce batch size as the flange gap closes.
8. Confirm at least two relative turns separately from any seating claim. Release only with established thread
   support, check actual jaw opening and unloading, withdraw gently along the axis, and inspect before finishing.

## General — Read results and recover without compounding errors

- Check reached pose, translation/orientation residuals, `ik_hit_limit`, saturation, and settling even when `ok`.
  Small IK error means a target is kinematically feasible; it does not establish a collision-free path.
- `holding` is a bilateral-load signal, not identity or engagement. `empty` can occur with a retained nut when
  one pad unloads; combine images, opening, contact history, and part motion rather than blindly opening or closing.
- Read `degrees_turned`, `regrasp_cycles`, `stop_phase`, and `stop_reason`; screw rotation is measured at the hand.
  `completed` does not prove advance; `timed_out` is incomplete. Wait for long calls; recover disconnect results with `last`.
- Early or repeated stalls require diagnosis. If available, `stall_at_start: false` only establishes prior motion.
  This episode used a sustained torque rise above baseline, not the old 0.15 Nm budget; use README thresholds.
- `lost_grasp`: locate and support the nut, then recenter before reclosing. `loaded_on_open`: fingers may still
  be hooked; do not reset through contact. With independent support, gently open and withdraw axially to clear them.
- Earlier recoveries found approximately 0.5–1 mm transverse corrections could restore opposing contact.
  Recenter gently instead of squeezing harder; large loads or collapsed opening can mean a wedge at the inner edge.
- A blocked sweep at similar roll angles, with freer rotation away from the fixture, suggests interference.
  Retain support, retreat safely, change posture/roll in clear space, and realign; do not repeat obstructed sweeps.
- If engaged, back off with matched negative roll/feed rather than pulling straight out. A nut perched on the
  tip can fall when the hand clears; unsupported release is never an engagement test. Avoid tilting unsecured parts.
- For a handoff, verify the receiver's outer-flat grip before releasing the donor, then remeasure bore alignment.

## ROV two-hand — Freely turning bolt and backup retention

- If the scene has a loose bolt, verify insertion through both holes and retain its head with the backup hand.
  Establish head contact before driving the nut and maintain restraint through working-hand release/reset cycles.
- Watch both nut and bolt: common rotation can consume hand/world nut turns with little relative advance.
  Estimate nut-minus-bolt rotation and matching relative feed; commanded feed cannot compensate for a spinning bolt.
- If the backup slips, stop, preserve support, and reacquire the head. Nut attachment alone does not justify backup release.
  These are conditional precautions, not outcomes demonstrated by this one-hand transcript.

## ROV one-hand — Scene clearance and recovery limits

- Remeasure each ROV scene. The pipe, neighboring fasteners, vehicle frame, foam, thrusters, and camera hardware
  constrain the whole arm; a free wrist roll away from the fixture does not guarantee a clear approach or reset.
- Historical fingertips extended about 34 mm beyond the TCP; verify current geometry. No collision avoidance was supplied.
- Sand can catch drops, but retrieval may be difficult; do not assume a recovery table.

## Tabletop — Transfer limits

- Use the tabletop's own frame, cameras, gravity/support geometry, rates, and reachable paths. A table may support
  recovery only after observed stable contact; neither tabletop poses nor ROV roll choices transfer between scenes.
