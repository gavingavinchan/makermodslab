# General — Nut-threading operating notes

These notes are advisory; the next workspace's README governs commands, sensors, limits, and profiles.

## General — Success, seating, and evidence

- Success requires **at least two relative nut-minus-bolt turns while threaded**; seating is separate.
  Requested roll, measured hand roll, world nut rotation, and relative thread rotation are different quantities.
- Track part rotation and axial advance relative to the bolt. With verified 3.5 mm pitch, two relative turns
  imply about 7 mm advance before seating; commanded TCP feed and regrasp counts do not prove that advance.
- Seating requires the nut's bearing face to reach the flange. Measure that gap along the bolt axis;
  an outer-face estimate needs independently measured nut thickness and correctly identified surfaces.
- Recheck face identity from another view: bevels, fingers, and background can corrupt depth samples.
  If the bearing gap is occluded or measurement uncertainty could hide millimetres, report seating as uncertain.
- Collision, hooked fingers, joint limits, or a loaded/compliant wrist can resist roll and raise torque,
  mimicking a snug stall before flange contact. Even a sustained torque-rise stall cannot certify seating alone.
- Combine gap, verified advance, clearance, loads, and reached motion. Retention after release proves neither
  flange contact nor the turn count. Scorer values are retrospective, not live sensors; `done` records a claim.

## General — Prepare a reliable first attempt

1. Look before each decision and after each motion. Locate nut, bolt tip/shaft, flange bearing face, and clear
   approach corridor; establish insertion/support needs. Use another view when a hand occludes the work.
2. Measure opposing face edges for nut center and three non-collinear points on one face for its normal.
   Sample shaft/tip and flange separately. Refresh after motion; depth queries may advance simulation time.
3. Grip opposing outer flats with sufficient pad overlap, avoiding bore and inner lip. Align the actual bore
   with both bolt and working roll axis; wrist alignment alone is insufficient. Remeasure after handoffs.
4. Choose reachable staging with wrist margin and clearance through turning, jaw opening, reset, and withdrawal.
   Include fingertips, camera, forearm, and other arm; test small rolls in clear space before approaching.
5. Approach from tip toward flange with near-parallel axes and preferably submillimetre centering.
   Use 1–2 mm final nudges; compare requested and reached motion. Stop at unexpected resistance or contact loss.
6. Touch the tip without forcing it. Verify signs in README; feed = relative degrees × pitch / 360.
7. Inspect a short starting stroke for relative rotation and matching advance before automatic reset cycles.
   Extend batches only after consistent progress and clear resets; shorten them as the bearing gap closes.
8. Verify the two-turn requirement independently of seating. Release only with established part support;
   inspect actual opening/unloading, withdraw gently with clearance, and check the retained assembly.

## ROV two-hand — Backup retention and common rotation

- In this profile the bolt head is axially retained but the bolt can turn in its holes. Axial retention is
  not rotational restraint: verify insertion through both holes and grip opposite head flats before driving.
- Maintain left-hand backup throughout right-hand opening/reset/regrasp. A straight-on left approach failed
  here; an oblique head grip was reachable. Verify both pads touch the head, not flange or neighboring hardware.
- A fixed commanded pose or `holding` signal does not prove a fixed bolt. Observe head orientation and nut
  advance; common nut/bolt rotation can consume hand or world nut turns with little relative thread progress.
- Estimate nut-minus-bolt rotation using a common axis/sign convention. Hand-based feed assumes effective
  restraint; if the head follows, stop and restore backup contact instead of forcing nominal axial feed.
- Keep head support while clearing the working hand; reacquire slipped backup. Release it only after tightening stops and support is established.
- Finite backup-arm compliance favors short gentle finishing strokes, such as 20° at 5°/s if README supports it.
  Relative hand speed is this profile's stall cue, not direct relative part motion or proof of seating.

## General — Diagnose and recover without compounding errors

- Read reached motion, residuals, limits, saturation, and settling even when `ok`; small IK error proves neither arrival nor clearance.
- In these profiles `holding` means bilateral load, not part identity/engagement; `empty` may mean one pad unloaded.
  Combine images, actual opening, loads, and part motion. Earlier 0.5–1 mm corrections sometimes restored contact.
- Read `degrees_turned`, `regrasp_cycles`, `stop_phase`, and `stop_reason`. `completed` is hand-motion completion;
  `timed_out` is incomplete. Wait for long calls; use README's result-recovery command after disconnects.
- For `lost_grasp`, locate/support the nut and restore outer-flat overlap before continuing. For `loaded_on_open`,
  verify actual unloading before wrist reset. With independent support, gently clear hooked fingers axially; stop if blocked.
- If still engaged, back off using matched negative relative roll/feed rather than pulling the nut straight out.
  Unsupported release is not an engagement test. For handoffs, confirm receiver support before freeing donor;
  avoid tilting unsecured parts, remeasure the bore afterward, and restore backup before tightening again.

## ROV two-hand — Clearance and release (also relevant to ROV one-hand)

- Check pipe, neighboring fasteners, frame, foam, thrusters, cameras, both arms, and fingertip overhang
  (historically about 34 mm beyond TCP). Do not assume collision avoidance or easy sand retrieval.
- Full opening can increase fixture contact: backup release here produced about 200 N on one pad and a blocked
  withdrawal. Even nearly full opening and `empty` do not mean clear; inspect loads and choose a clear retreat.
