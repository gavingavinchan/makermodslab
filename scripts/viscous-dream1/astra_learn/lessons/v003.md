# General — Nut-threading operating notes

These notes are advisory; the next workspace's README governs commands, sensors, limits, and profiles.

## General — Success, seating, and evidence

- Task success requires **at least two relative nut-minus-bolt turns while threaded**; seating is separate.
  Requested roll, measured hand roll, world nut rotation, and relative thread rotation are different quantities.
- Track nut motion relative to the bolt and axial advance along its axis. With verified 3.5 mm pitch,
  two relative turns imply about 7 mm advance before seating; TCP feed alone is not evidence of that advance.
- Seating requires the nut's bearing face to reach the flange after verified thread advance. Inspect that gap
  directly where possible; outer-face distance requires independently measured nut thickness along the axis.
- Collision, hooked fingers, joint limits, or a loaded/compliant wrist can mimic a snug torque stall.
  Check clearance, reached motion, loads, and the gap together; if the gap is occluded, report seating as uncertain.
- Scorer values are retrospective, not live sensors. `done` records a claim. Retention/protrusion alone cannot certify seating.

## General — Prepare a reliable first attempt

1. Look before each decision and after each motion. Locate the nut, bolt tip/shaft, flange bearing face, and
   approach corridor; establish whether the bolt needs insertion or restraint. Use another view for occlusion.
2. Measure opposing face edges for the nut center and three non-collinear points on one face for its normal.
   Sample shaft/tip and flange separately; bore depth can hit a bevel, finger, or background. Refresh after motion;
   depth queries may advance time. Compare the same surfaces along the bolt axis.
3. Grip opposing outer flats with adequate pad overlap, avoiding the bore and inner lip. Align the actual bore
   with the bolt and working wrist roll axis; wrist alignment alone is insufficient. Remeasure after any handoff.
4. Choose staging postures with reach, wrist margin, and clearance through approach, tightening, opening, and reset.
   Include fingertips, camera, forearm, and the other arm. Test small rolls in clear space before approaching.
5. Approach from the tip toward the flange with near-parallel axes and preferably submillimetre centering.
   Use 1–2 mm final nudges; compare requested and reached motion. Stop at unexpected resistance or contact loss.
6. Touch the leading face to the tip without forcing it. Verify handedness/signs in README; feed = relative degrees × pitch / 360.
   For the documented tip-side convention with a stationary bolt and 3.5 mm pitch, +20° pairs with +0.194 mm feed; inspect progress.
7. Confirm relative rotation and matching advance before repeated release/reset cycles. Start with a short,
   inspected screw batch; extend only after consistent progress and a clear reset. Shorten batches near the flange.
8. Verify the two-turn requirement separately from seating. Release the working hand only with established
   support; check actual opening/unloading, withdraw gently along the axis, and inspect the retained assembly.

## ROV two-hand — Backup grip and common rotation

- In the loose-bolt profile, verify insertion through both holes and grip opposite head flats with the left hand
  before driving the right-hand nut. Maintain backup contact throughout working-hand opening and regrasp cycles.
- An axial backup-hand approach may be unreachable; a reachable oblique grip across head flats worked here.
  Verify both pads contact the head rather than the flange or adjacent hardware; do not copy the old posture.
- A fixed commanded backup pose or `holding` signal does not prove an immobile bolt. Watch head orientation,
  both hands, and nut advance: common nut/bolt rotation can consume world or hand turns with little thread progress.
- Estimate relative nut-minus-bolt rotation using a common axis/sign convention. Commanded hand-based feed assumes
  effective restraint; if the bolt follows, stop and restore backup contact instead of forcing the nominal feed.
- Keep the head retained while clearing the working hand. If backup slips, preserve support and reacquire it.
  Release backup only after tightening stops and the assembly is supported.
- This profile has finite backup-arm compliance. Prefer short gentle finishing strokes, such as 20° at 5°/s
  when supported by README. Relative hand speed is a profile-specific stall cue, not direct part-motion sensing.

## General — Diagnose and recover without compounding errors

- Read reached pose, residuals, `ik_hit_limit`, saturation, and settling even when `ok`. Small IK error establishes
  neither a collision-free path nor actual arrival. Repeated blocked sweeps call for clearance diagnosis, not force.
- `holding` means bilateral load, not part identity or engagement; `empty` can mean one pad unloaded.
  Combine images, actual opening, loads, and part motion. Recenter gently rather than repeatedly squeezing harder.
- Earlier 0.5–1 mm transverse corrections sometimes restored contact. High loads or collapsed opening can indicate wedging.
- Read `degrees_turned`, `regrasp_cycles`, `stop_phase`, and `stop_reason`; screw degrees are hand motion.
  `completed` does not certify thread advance; `timed_out` is incomplete. Wait for long calls; use `last` after disconnects.
- For `lost_grasp`, locate/support the nut and restore outer-flat contact. For `loaded_on_open`, verify unloading
  before reset. With independent support, gently clear hooked fingers axially; inspect each step and stop if blocked.
- Maximum opening sometimes increased fixture contact load here. Retreat safely and change roll/posture in clear space.
- If still engaged, back off with matched negative relative roll/feed rather than pulling the nut straight out.
  Unsupported release is not an engagement test. For handoffs, confirm receiver support before freeing the donor;
  avoid tilting unsecured parts, remeasure the bore afterward, and restore backup before restarting tightening.
