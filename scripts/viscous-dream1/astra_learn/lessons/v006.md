# General — Nut-threading operating notes

These notes are advisory; the next workspace's README governs current commands, sensors, limits, and profiles.

## General — Prepare a reliable first attempt

1. Look before decisions and after motions. Locate nut, bolt, flange bearing face, support, and approach clearance.
2. Measure opposing nut edges for center and three non-collinear points on one face for its normal; identify shaft/tip separately.
   Refresh observations after movement; depth queries may advance simulation time. Change views when surfaces are occluded.
3. Grip opposing outer flats with adequate pad overlap, avoiding the bore and inner lip. Align the actual bore
   with the bolt and working roll axis; wrist alignment alone is insufficient. Remeasure after handoffs.
4. Choose reachable staging with wrist margin through turning, opening, reset, and withdrawal. Include fingertips,
   camera, forearm, and the other arm; test small rolls in clear space. Do not assume a collision-avoiding planner.
5. Approach from tip toward flange with near-parallel axes and preferably submillimetre centering; use 1–2 mm final nudges.
   Compare requested and reached motion. Stop at unexpected resistance or contact loss; do not force the tip into the bore.
6. Verify thread pitch, approach, and signs in README. Nominal feed = relative degrees × pitch / 360.
7. Inspect a short starting stroke for part rotation and matching axial advance before automatic reset cycles.
   Extend batches only after consistent progress and clear resets; shorten them as the bearing gap closes.

## ROV two-hand — Backup retention and common rotation

- In this backup profile the head is axially retained but the bolt can rotate. Verify insertion through both holes,
  then secure opposite head flats with the left gripper before driving the nut with the right.
- The attempted axial head approach hit reach/orientation limits; a reachable oblique approach worked in this scene.
  Choose geometry anew, orient jaws in clear space, and verify contact with the head itself rather than nearby hardware.
- Keep backup through every right-hand opening, wrist reset, and regrasp, and while clearing the working hand.
  If backup slips or a handoff needs that hand, stop driving, establish support, and restore restraint before resuming.
- Fixed hand commands and `holding` do not prove a stationary bolt. Observe head orientation and nut advance;
  common nut/bolt rotation can consume hand or world nut turns with little relative thread progress.
- Use a common axis/sign for nut-minus-bolt rotation. Hand-based feed assumes restraint; restore backup if the head follows.
- Gentle finishing strokes, such as 20° at 5°/s when supported, limit backup-arm windup. This profile's relative
  hand-speed stall cue comes from encoders/kinematics; it is neither direct relative part rotation nor a seating sensor.

## General — Success, seating, and honest evidence

- Task success requires **at least two relative nut-minus-bolt turns while threaded**. Requested roll, measured
  gripped-hand roll, world nut rotation, and relative thread rotation are distinct; reset/regrasp counts prove none of them.
- Track axial advance relative to the bolt as well as part rotation. At verified 3.5 mm pitch, two relative turns
  imply about 7 mm advance before seating. TCP feed alone does not prove that advance or engagement.
- Seating concerns the nut's bearing face meeting the flange. Estimate the gap along the bolt axis; an outer-face
  estimate needs independently measured nut thickness and correctly identified surfaces. A protruding tip does not prove seating.
- Cross-check another view: bevels, fingers, and background can corrupt depth samples. If uncertainty or occlusion
  could hide millimetres, report seating uncertain. Retention after release establishes neither seating nor turn count.
- Collision, hooked fingers, joint limits, or a loaded/compliant wrist can resist roll and raise torque before flange contact.
  Even a late sustained torque-rise stall cannot certify seating; combine gap, advance, clearance, loads, and reached motion.
- Scorer measurements are retrospective evidence, unavailable to the live operator. `done` records a claim, not certification.

## General — Diagnose and recover without compounding errors

- Read reached motion, residuals, limits, saturation, and settling even when `ok`; small IK error proves neither arrival nor clearance.
- Where `holding` is based on bilateral load, it proves neither part identity nor engagement; `empty` may mean one pad unloaded.
  Combine images, actual jaw opening, loads, and part motion. Repeated closing alone may not restore a useful grasp.
- Small 0.5–1 mm centering corrections can restore contact; determine direction anew and avoid forcing a threaded nut sideways.
  If corrections do not improve contact, reassess pad overlap, support, and obstruction rather than repeating them blindly.
- Read hand degrees, regrasp count, and stop phase/reason; `timed_out` is incomplete. Wait for active calls; recover lost results via README.
- For `lost_grasp`, locate/support the nut and restore grip; it need not mean thread disengagement. For `loaded_on_open`,
  inspect actual opening and unloading before reset. With support, gently clear hooked fingers; stop if withdrawal is blocked.
- Repeated stops at the same wrist angle near a fixture, with free rotation away, suggest a blocked sweep.
  Retain support, change entry roll in clear space, and realign instead of adding torque.
- If engaged, back off with matched negative relative roll/feed rather than pulling straight off. Never test engagement by
  unsupported release; confirm receiver support before freeing a donor, and never tilt an unsecured nut.
