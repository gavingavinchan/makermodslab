## General — Observe and plan before moving

- Look before decisions and after motions. Refresh depth observations after movement; queries may advance time.
- Identify the actual part surfaces, tip, collar underside, receiving surfaces, and support separately.
  Cross-check views: a depth pick can hit a finger, bevel, near cylinder surface, or background through a hole.
- Infer a hole center from multiple visible rim points and its plane, not one point in the opening.
  Estimate the pin axis from its geometry; neither the TCP nor one cylindrical surface point is its centerline.
- Plan the whole grasp, transfer, descent, turn, opening, reset, and withdrawal before committing.
  Include distal fingertips beyond the TCP, gripper body, camera, forearm, fixture, table, and parked arm.
- Motions are not necessarily collision planned. Orient in clear space with wrist margin; check reach and tip clearance together.

## Shackle tabletop — Grip for a reliable first attempt

- Measure pin-to-tool offset and tip protrusion after grasping; remeasure after any slip or regrasp.
  A vertical tool does not establish a vertical, centered pin or a suitable screw axis.
- Choose opposing contact surfaces with enough pad overlap. Verify both loads, actual opening, visible
  part identity, and part motion during a small lift before a long transfer.
- The initial grasp's attempted lift exceeded reach; joint-limit warnings and tilted transfers followed.
  A lower shank grasp improved reachable staging in this attempt, but did not produce verified insertion.
- Balance that reach benefit against fingers reaching the ear before the tip reaches the lower thread.
  Plan a supported change to the head before clearance runs out; do not improvise it while loaded at the ear.
- An initial head grasp needs suitable overlap, alignment, reach, and sweep clearance; this episode did not validate it.
- Regrasp on stable table support if clearance is inadequate; standing on the tip alone does not guarantee stability.

## Shackle tabletop — Align and establish passage

- Align the actual pin axis with both ears before descent. Clear the upper rim before translating over it.
- Use small, inspected final nudges, typically 1–2 mm; use smaller directed corrections near contact.
  Aim for submillimetre centering where observations support it, without treating that as guaranteed accuracy.
- At resistance, stop and inspect reached motion, pin tilt, tip location, and finger/fixture clearance.
  Unload or retract along a clear path before lateral correction; do not drag a constrained tip sideways.
- Remeasure rim center and pin offset instead of pushing or searching blindly; this attempt never established insertion.
- Verify that the tip actually passes the upper ear from complementary views and depth geometry.
  Overlap in one view or a lower tool position is insufficient; passing the upper ear does not engage the lower one.

## Shackle tabletop — Support before release or regrasp

- Establish support that prevents falling and tipping before opening. Mere contact with an ear is insufficient;
  do not test uncertain insertion or engagement by unsupported release. The right arm cannot provide backup.
- If ear support cannot be established, retain the grip and return to a stable, reachable table placement.
  Keep recovery away from edges and leave room for the next grasp and its withdrawal.
- Open only as needed under the current README, inspecting actual opening and both jaw loads as it settles.
  A release command or `empty` label does not mean the fingers are unloaded or clear.
- Here, opening was followed by large jaw loads; even `empty` coincided with roughly 330 N on one pad.
  Withdrawal then had large tracking error and the pin was lost. These are warning signs, not a safe release recipe.
- If opening remains loaded, retain or restore support and reassess contact before resetting or withdrawing.
  Hooking, collision, and stored load are possible explanations; the exact ejection mechanism is unproven.
- Verify regrasp by observed pin motion and remeasure its axis; closure, wrist motion, or a stall alone proves nothing.

## Shackle tabletop — Threading and seating criteria

- The README recorded in the transcript specifies a right-hand 2.5 mm pitch and clockwise tightening viewed from the head.
  Recheck the next README's signs; nominal axial feed is relative degrees × pitch / 360 along the pin axis.
- Once passage and lower-thread alignment are established, inspect a short starting turn for pin rotation
  and matching axial advance relative to the fixed shackle. TCP feed and wrist roll alone prove neither.
- Use screw/reset cycles only after thread-start evidence, bilateral grip, and support; inspect stop reasons and short batches.
- Task success is collar seating on the upper ear, not a turn-count threshold. Shorten strokes near contact.
  Cross-check the collar underside against the upper bearing surface with the pin still aligned and engaged.
- A stall, torque rise, retention after release, or signed gap alone cannot certify seating. Report uncertainty
  if occlusion prevents verification. No tightening rate or finishing sequence was validated by this attempt.

## General — Diagnose and recover without compounding errors

- Read actual residuals, orientation error, limits, saturation, and settling even when `ok` is true.
  A good IK solution does not prove reached motion, clearance, or useful contact; change staging after repeated stops.
- `holding` reflects contact, not part identity or secure alignment; `empty` may mean only one pad unloaded.
  Repeated closing or more torque is not a substitute for inspecting pad overlap, obstruction, and support.
- If genuinely threaded and backing out, match reverse relative roll with reverse pitch feed; do not pull straight out.
  After a drop, locate and observe the settled pin before recovery; replan its grasp and orientation from scratch.
- Wait for active commands; recover lost results via README. `done` is a claim: report verified progress and uncertainty honestly.
