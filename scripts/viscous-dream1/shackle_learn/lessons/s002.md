# General — Shackle pin operating notes

These notes are advisory; the next workspace's README governs commands, sensing, limits, and capabilities.

## Shackle tabletop — Objective and outcome evidence

- Use only the left arm; the right is parked and disabled. The shackle and holder are fixed to the table.
- Pass the threaded end through the upper ear, engage the lower thread, and tighten until the collar rests on the upper ear.
  Upper-ear passage, lower-thread engagement, relative advance, and seating are distinct.
- This supplied attempt succeeded: final inserted, engaged, and seated were true; ever_inserted and
  ever_engaged were also true. The scorer recorded one engagement, about 8.038 thread turns, and two drops.
- The supplied outcome supersedes the older failure account. The final completion claim agrees with
  measured task state; `claim_vs_truth` says “No disagreement detected in the final claim (wording heuristic).”
  That heuristic is not semantic proof of every assertion, the visual verification, or intermediate claims.
- Scorer data are retrospective evidence, not live operator sensors. A signed collar-to-ear gap alone
  establishes neither insertion nor seating: a loose pin elsewhere can produce a misleading gap.
- Eventual success supports the achieved result, not the reliability of every maneuver or inferred cause.

## General — Observe and plan before moving

- Look before decisions and after motions; refresh depth observations after movement. Queries may advance time.
- Identify the pin, tip, collar underside, ear bearing surfaces, bore edges, and support separately.
  Cross-check views: depth picks can hit fingers, bevels, near cylinder surfaces, or background through a hole.
- Estimate the bore axis from multiple boundary points and depth transitions, not one point in the opening.
  Estimate the pin centerline from its geometry; neither the TCP nor a single surface point is its axis.
- Plan grasp, lift, transfer, descent, head access, turning, jaw opening/reset, and withdrawal together.
  Include fingertips beyond the TCP, gripper body, camera, forearm, fixture, table, and parked arm.
- Motion is not necessarily collision planned. Orient in clear space and preserve wrist travel for turning.
  Check reach and pin/finger clearance throughout the route, not just at the pickup and destination.

## Shackle tabletop — Grip for a reliable first attempt

- Choose opposing surfaces with adequate pad overlap; verify opening, both loads, part identity, and pin motion in a test lift.
- Measure pin-to-tool offset, tilt, and tip protrusion after grasping, slip, or regrasping; a vertical tool need not mean a vertical pin.
- The low initial grasp met resistance, attributed by the operator to fingers reaching the ear too early.
  The higher grasp passed a lift test but its transfer lift hit reach limits; a head grasp is not a universal fix.
- A revised intermediate grasp eventually allowed passage. Select height by reachable tip clearance above
  the rim and remaining finger clearance during descent; these observations do not supply a fixed grasp recipe.
- Plan a supported head regrasp before clearance runs out; otherwise return to stable table support and revise the grasp.
- Standing on the threaded tip alone is not reliable support: an adjustment tipped the pin onto the table.

## Shackle tabletop — Align and establish passage

- Align the actual pin axis with both ears. Lift clear of the rim before translating over it.
- Use inspected final nudges around 1–2 mm, reducing increments near contact; correct only a measured offset.
- At resistance, inspect reached motion, tilt, tip position, grip, and finger/fixture clearance.
  Unload or retract along a clear path before lateral correction; do not drag a constrained tip sideways.
- Repeated rim contact prompted moving the pin aside and resampling the exposed bore. The operator revised
  its estimated center by about 2.3 mm; subsequent descent progressed. Miscentering is a supported hypothesis,
  not proof that every earlier stop had the same cause. Remeasure rather than repeat blind searches.
- Check tip passage below the upper ear in complementary views; descent, overlap, good TCP residuals, or resistance are insufficient.
- Passage through the upper ear may guide the pin but does not prove lower-thread engagement or axial support.

## Shackle tabletop — Support before release or regrasp

- Establish support against falling and tipping before opening; mere ear contact is insufficient.
  Do not test uncertain insertion or engagement by unsupported release. The right arm cannot provide backup.
- During the first ear-to-head transition, opening coincided with a roughly 25 mm detected drop; the scorer
  recorded engagement immediately afterward, before the first commanded tightening turn. This successful
  continuation does not validate a controlled, supported handoff or deliberate drop-to-engage procedure.
- If support is uncertain, retain the grip and return to stable, reachable table support when safely possible.
- Inspect opening, both loads, and withdrawal clearance. Earlier table placement showed roughly 140 N on one jaw despite `empty`.
- Later threading stopped with `loaded_on_open`. Jaw/shackle contact was the operator's explanation,
  not independently established by the outcome. Inspect and unload contact before resetting or withdrawing.
- After established threading, clearing and regrasping higher on the head was followed by further advance
  and eventual seating. Verify renewed pad contact and actual pin motion; closure or wrist motion alone is no proof.

## Shackle tabletop — Thread advance and seating

- The recorded README specifies a right-hand 2.5 mm pitch, clockwise viewed from the head. Recheck signs
  in the next README; nominal feed is relative degrees × pitch / 360 along the pin axis.
- After passage and lower-thread alignment, use a short starting turn and inspect pin rotation plus axial
  advance relative to the fixed shackle. Wrist roll, commanded feed, and TCP descent alone prove neither.
- This successful episode used initial 30° and 60° turns with pitch-matched feed at 15°/s, then screw cycles.
  These are examples after alignment, not a guarantee of thread start or a requirement to repeat the sequence.
- Use automatic release/reset/regrasp only with thread-start evidence, bilateral contact, and support.
  Read stop reason and phase; inspect short batches. Reported wrist degrees are not measured relative thread turns.
- Shorten batches near the collar; half-turns at 10°/s were used here. The final stall and changed jaw contact did not prove seating.
- Success means the collar underside rests on the upper ear with the pin correctly inserted and engaged,
  not a turn-count threshold. Inspect from complementary views with the gripper clear only after safe release.
  Account for collar thickness; its top is not the seating surface. Retention, torque rise, or gap alone is insufficient.

## General — Diagnose and recover without compounding errors

- Read residuals, orientation error, limits, saturation, and settling despite `ok`; good IK does not prove clearance or reached motion.
- `holding` indicates contact, not secure alignment or part identity; `empty` need not mean both jaws are unloaded.
- After a drop, locate the settled pin and replan in clear space. Staged recovery helped; repeated unreachable rotations did not.
- If genuinely threaded and backing out, match reverse relative roll with reverse pitch feed; do not pull straight out.
- Wait for active commands and recover lost results via the README. Report verified progress and uncertainty;
  `done` records a claim, not independent certification. If views cannot establish seating, say so.
